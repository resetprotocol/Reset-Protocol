# The Reset Protocol

Blockchain-verified community health surveillance for healthcare deserts.

## The Problem

Buffalo's healthcare desert leaves older adults with:
- **68% Vitamin D deficiency** (undetected)
- **54% elevated blood pressure** (unmanaged)
- **41% pre-diabetic/diabetic** (unaware)
- Fragmented health records scattered across systems
- No real-time public health data for resource allocation

## The Solution

Community-based health screenings with blockchain-verified records that give patients:
- **Self-sovereign health identity** (they own their data, not hospitals)
- **Permanent, tamper-proof records** accessible via QR code
- **Real-time community health surveillance** for public health agencies
- **Geographic heat maps** showing health disparities by zip code

## Current Status

**80 patients screened** (October-November 2024)  
**Deploying on Base blockchain**  
**Patent pending** (provisional filing in progress)  
**Pilot location:** Buffalo, NY (Erie County)  

## Key Findings

From our first 80 community screenings:
- 68% have Vitamin D levels below 20 ng/mL (deficient)
- 54% have blood pressure above 130/80 (elevated)
- 41% have A1C above 5.7% (pre-diabetic or diabetic)
- West Side Buffalo shows 81% Vitamin D deficiency vs 54% citywide

## Technology Stack

- **Blockchain:** Base (Ethereum Layer 2)
- **Smart Contracts:** Thirdweb
- **Data Collection:** Google Sheets → CSV
- **Visualization:** Google My Maps (heat maps)
- **Patient Interface:** QR code health wallets

## Why Blockchain?

Traditional medical records:
-Scattered across multiple systems  
-Inaccessible to patients  
-Lost when switching providers  
Can be altered or deleted  

Blockchain health records:
-Patient-owned and controlled  
-Permanent and tamper-proof  
-Accessible anywhere via smartphone  
-Privacy-protected (anonymous patient IDs)  

## Impact

This infrastructure enables:
- **Uninsured seniors** to have verified health records (no insurance card needed)
- **Emergency responders** to access critical health data during crises
- **Public health agencies** to see real-time community health patterns
- **Researchers** to identify clinical trial participants instantly
- **Other cities** to replicate the model in their healthcare deserts
